The breeding clock

  1. Week 0Heavy rain fills drains, tyres and containers.
  2. Weeks 1–2Aedes aegypti eggs mature into adult mosquitoes in 7 to 14 days.
  3. Weeks 2–4Viral incubation, then symptoms and hospital admissions.

Spraying after admissions rise is already weeks behind. This tool moves the decision to the rain.

From weather to ward risk in three steps

Every forecast is built only from data available on the day it is made, then checked on years the model never saw.

1

Read the local weather

Weekly rainfall, average temperature and relative humidity for each of the 60 wards.

  • Rainfall (mm)
  • Avg temp (°C)
  • Humidity (%)
2

Shift it by the breeding lag

Rain is lagged 0 to 8 weeks and summed over 2, 4 and 8 weeks. Cases follow rain most closely about 3 weeks later.

3

Weigh ward vulnerability

A gradient-boosted model combines the lags with drainage bottlenecks, crowding and waste collection, then assigns a risk tier with an 80% range.

  • Water-logging index
  • Population density
  • Sanitation score
